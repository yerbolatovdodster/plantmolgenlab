spotlight_text: 'To find genes that help wheat adapt to Kazakhstan''s conditions, we work with the UK nested association mapping (NAM) population from the John Innes Centre. Its common parent, the UK spring cultivar Paragon, was crossed with 24 donors of diverse origin. The genome of each recombinant inbred line (RIL) is a mosaic of Paragon and donor segments, so NAM combines the resolution of GWAS with the power of classical QTL mapping.


  We tested 290 lines from 24 families in 2019–2022 in two contrasting regions: the north (Barayev Scientific-Production Center for Grain Farming, Akmola region) and the southeast (Kazakh Research Institute of Agriculture and Plant Growing, Almaty region). GWAS with 10,448 polymorphic SNPs identified 74 QTL for heading and maturity time, plant height and peduncle length [[1]](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants13182623), and 72 QTL for yield components, from spike length to thousand-kernel weight and yield per m² [[2]](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy14081848). Several lines outperformed local checks for kernel number, kernel weight and yield making them promising material for breeding [[3]](https://doi.org/10.26577/eje.2021.v67.i2.06).'

We genotype collections on SNP arrays, map genomic regions linked to traits and develop markers for selection. The lab has co-developed 10 cultivars of wheat, barley and soybean.
