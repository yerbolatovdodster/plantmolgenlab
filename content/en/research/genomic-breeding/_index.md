- title: Genetic dissection of adaptation and yield traits in a wheat NAM population
  image: images/research/wheat-nam-en.svg
  text: 'To find genes that help wheat adapt to Kazakhstan''s conditions, we work with the UK nested association mapping (NAM) population from the [John Innes Centre](https://jic.ac.uk/). Its common parent, the UK spring cultivar Paragon, was crossed with 24 donors of diverse origin. The genome of each recombinant inbred line (RIL) is a mosaic of Paragon and donor segments, so NAM combines the resolution of GWAS with the power of classical QTL mapping.


    We are testing 290 lines in two contrasting regions: the north (Barayev Scientific-Production Center for Grain Farming, Akmola region) and the southeast (Kazakh Research Institute of Agriculture and Plant Growing, Almaty region). By combining field phenotyping with SNP genotyping, we map genomic regions that control adaptation traits, such as heading and maturity time and plant height, and yield components, and select promising lines for breeding.


    For more information, follow [Dr. Akerke Amalova''s ResearchGate page](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Akerke-Amalova).'
- title: Kazakhstan's soybeans in the global germplasm
  image: images/research/soybean-collection-en.svg
  text: 'Soybean is one of the fastest-growing crops in Kazakhstan: its area expanded from about 71,000 ha in 2011 to over 200,000 ha by 2021, mostly in the Almaty region. To understand where local cultivars come from and how much diversity they carry, we resequenced the genomes of Kazakhstan''s cultivars and breeding lines and compared them with a global collection of 694 accessions: modern cultivars from Europe, East Asia and the Americas, Chinese landraces and wild soybean (*Glycine soja*).


    Kazakhstan''s soybeans are genetically closest to European and North American cultivars, reflecting cultivar exchange and breeding for temperate climates, but they have the narrowest genetic base in the collection. This points to two breeding strategies: broadening the base with diverse foreign and wild germplasm, and accumulating favourable alleles already present in locally adapted lines.


    For more information, see [Zatybekov et al., *BMC Genomics*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-025-12024-8).'
